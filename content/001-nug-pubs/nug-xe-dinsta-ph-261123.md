## Realism as Interesting in Modern Art?

This  series of words seem to help discuss **composition** in modern art:

- Honesty
- disconcerting
-  balance
-  realism 
- tension
-  interesting
- realism

Reflections with a student about a Georgia O'Keefe painting [[h-georgia-okeefe-landscape-1952]]. 

There is the idea that awkwardness carries a degree of honesty as it is unfettered and lacks some polishing filter before presentation. 

In this light, awkward becomes synonymous with honesty in art talk. I brought up Van Gogh as an example. 

In common parlance, outside of art speak, reality gets equated with honesty: Fiction is telling a lie.

But doesn't art walk a different walk. Aren't we inspired by these constructions and un-true stories. Afterall a painting is never a real thing. It is always just paint that appears as something else. 

The subject of "interesting" seems to break open these perspectives.

When you go to make a "realistic" or "honest" image it appears uninteresting as with the memories of your black and white photographs. The uninterest lets you down? 

Doesn't it appear that with this logic that  "Interesting" is a form of the realistic? At least in the language of art? If we are in a bit of a Frankenstein scenario convincing ourselves that the inert can speak to us and our communities?

We talked about tension and balance in the composition. You felt that the blue strip of sky was more disconcerting than interesting. 

But I know from working with you that you are not one to care for light answers or frivolity and that you are looking for deep meaning in art and art processes. Art isn't just something pretty for you but at the same time you don't want it to be disconcerting? I am very curious to know more about the lines you have drawn in the sand about art.
